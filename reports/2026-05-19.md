## Evaluating Deep Research Agents on Expert Consulting Work: A Benchmark with Verifiers, Rubrics, and Cognitive Traps

- ID: `2605.17554v1`
- Score: 9.05
- Themes: End-to-End AI Scientists, Literature And Survey Agents
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17554v1

A new benchmark evaluates deep research agents (DRAs) on structured, multi-document tasks typical of expert consulting work. Testing three leading agents—Claude Opus 4.6, OpenAI o3, and Google Gemini 3.1—revealed uniformly low acceptance rates and distinct failure patterns, such as fabrication and arithmetic errors. The study highlights the need for stricter evaluation frameworks as DRAs are deployed in enterprise settings.

**Why it matters today:** This benchmark is timely as enterprises increasingly rely on AI for complex research and decision-making tasks.


## Qumus: Realization of An Embodied AI Quantum Material Experimentalist

- ID: `2605.18407v1`
- Score: 8.65
- Themes: Closed-Loop Empirical Science
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18407v1

Qumus, the first embodied AI quantum materials experimentalist, autonomously performs the entire scientific process, from hypothesis generation to experimental execution and reporting. It has achieved milestones like AI-creation of graphene and fabrication of atomically thin nanodevices. This innovation sets a new benchmark for AI-driven discovery in quantum materials and electronics.

**Why it matters today:** This breakthrough highlights the growing role of AI in automating and accelerating scientific discovery, a critical need in today's fast-paced research landscape.


## STRIDE: A Self-Reflective Agent Framework for Reliable Automatic Equation Discovery

- ID: `2605.17790v1`
- Score: 8.65
- Themes: Closed-Loop Empirical Science
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17790v1

STRIDE is a novel framework for automatic equation discovery that leverages self-reflection and feedback loops to improve reliability and robustness. By combining data-aware generation, mixed-fitting evaluation, and repair mechanisms, it outperforms existing methods in accuracy and structural recovery. This advancement could significantly enhance symbolic regression and scientific modeling.

**Why it matters today:** This is particularly relevant as AI-driven scientific discovery continues to gain traction in diverse fields.


## FML-bench: A Controlled Study of AI Research Agent Strategies from the Perspective of Search Dynamics

- ID: `2605.17373v1`
- Score: 8.35
- Themes: End-to-End AI Scientists
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17373v1

FML-Bench is a new benchmark designed to evaluate AI research agents by isolating strategy from infrastructure and introducing process-level metrics. Testing six agents, it reveals that simpler strategies like greedy search can rival more complex ones under certain conditions. An adaptive agent leveraging these insights outperformed others, highlighting the importance of dynamic strategy switching. The benchmark is openly available for further exploration.

**Why it matters today:** This work is timely as AI-driven automation in research is becoming increasingly critical for accelerating scientific progress.


## Real-time Multi-instrument Autonomous Discovery of Novel Phase-change Memory Materials

- ID: `2605.18033v1`
- Score: 7.65
- Themes: Autonomous Discovery
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18033v1

Researchers unveiled the Multi-instrument Autonomous Discovery (MAD) framework, a system that integrates real-time data from multiple instruments to accelerate the discovery of novel materials. Applied to phase-change memory materials, MAD achieved a seven-fold speed-up in identifying promising candidates. This approach could transform autonomous labs by enabling simultaneous structural and functional optimization.

**Why it matters today:** This breakthrough highlights the growing importance of autonomous systems in accelerating scientific discovery, especially in materials science.
